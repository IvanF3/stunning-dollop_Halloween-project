Plan Before Going Out

If you plan to go trick-or-treating, agree on the route, group, and return time with a trusted adult before leaving. Children should go with a responsible adult or an agreed group, and everyone should know how to contact the accompanying adult if separated. Stay in familiar areas, use sidewalks where available, and cross roads carefully at appropriate crossings. Visit only homes that are clearly participating and respect people who do not wish to take part. Follow local guidance about event times and neighbourhood rules. If plans change, check with the responsible adult instead of wandering to a new location. Keeping the group together and communicating clearly helps prevent confusion. Make Costumes Easy to See and Move In

Costumes should allow people to walk, turn, see, and breathe comfortably. Check that hems do not drag on the ground and that shoes fit properly. Masks and headpieces should not block vision or make breathing difficult; face paint or a different design may be a better choice. If you will be outside after dark, reflective details and bright accessories can help drivers and pedestrians see you. A flashlight may be useful on routes with low lighting. Props should be lightweight and harmless. Before leaving, try walking in the costume and check that accessories stay in place. Comfort is part of good costume design. Decorate Without Creating Hazards

Decorations should create atmosphere without blocking safe movement. Keep steps, entrances, corridors, and sidewalks clear so people can move easily. Secure lightweight decorations so wind or accidental contact will not knock them down. Place cables where they cannot be tripped over, and follow instructions for electrical decorations. Battery-powered lights are useful for pumpkins and indoor displays because they avoid an open flame. Keep paper, fabric, and other flammable materials away from heat sources. Check Treats Thoughtfully

Treats are part of many Halloween celebrations, and care helps make them comfortable for everyone. Follow your family’s guidance about checking packaging and choosing what is suitable to eat. Keep food in its original packaging when ingredient information is needed. If you are handing out treats, choose items with intact packaging and consider a non-food alternative such as stickers or pencils. Be aware that allergies and food preferences differ. Do not pressure someone to accept a treat, and ask before giving homemade food. A friendly exchange should allow each person to decide what works for them. Be Considerate of Others

Not everyone celebrates Halloween, and people can have different comfort levels with noise, costumes, crowds, or spooky decorations. Respect homes that do not participate and follow local signs or instructions. Avoid frightening strangers, blocking entrances, or making noise late at night. Choose costumes that are considerate and do not mock someone’s culture, identity, or beliefs. At a party, make room for guests who prefer quieter activities. Ask permission before taking or sharing photographs of others. Kindness helps more people enjoy the event and makes the celebration memorable for the right reasons. Finally, remember that Halloween is a shared celebration for some people and not an event everyone chooses to join. Prepare for Weather and the Environment

Weather can affect outdoor Halloween activities, especially when costumes are light, roads are wet, or the evening is cold. Check the forecast and choose clothing that suits the conditions while allowing the costume to remain visible. If it is raining, be careful on slippery paths and keep electronic decorations protected according to their instructions. Avoid unfamiliar or poorly lit areas, and use a route agreed with a trusted adult. Reusable bags, containers, and decorations can reduce waste. Dispose of rubbish properly and follow local recycling or composting rules. A good plan considers the people attending and the place where the event happens. A Safe and Welcoming Celebration at Home

A home celebration can include pumpkin decorating, storytelling, themed crafts, games, music, and snacks. Set up activities so there is enough room to move around and keep exits clear. If children are using scissors, carving tools, hot equipment, or electrical decorations, adults should supervise the parts that require extra care. Keep food and craft supplies separate, and clean up spills quickly. Choose games that fit the ages and interests of guests, and offer quieter options for anyone who prefers them. A welcoming event allows guests to participate in different ways. Safety, inclusion, and creativity work best when planned together.
